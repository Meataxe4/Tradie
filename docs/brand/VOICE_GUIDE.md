Distilled from `Sorted_By_Style_and_Voice_Guide_v2.1.pdf` in this folder. The PDF is

This file exists so the rules can be read without opening a 4 MB PDF every session.

> Straight up. A little cheeky. Always sorted.
>
> Write and design for homeowners who have been burned by a tradie before. Give them
> one next step, the price in writing and the licence on show. Use the fewest words
> that leave them calm and certain.

Tagline: **Sorted before anyone knocks.**

---

## 1. Voice

We write like the tradie you'd recommend to your mum: turns up on time, gives a
straight price, explains it, cleans up after.

- **Turn up on time.** Lead with the point. The first line says what's happening or what to do.
- **Give a straight price.** Plain numbers, GST included, and what could change them. No fine print.
- **Explain it.** Ordinary words, one question at a time. Translate trade-speak. Never make anyone feel silly.
- **Clean up after.** Cut every word that isn't working. End with what happens next, or simply stop.
- **Keep a wink.** Cheek belongs in headlines, empty states and the sorted moment.
  **Never near money, safety or disputes.**

Three tones: **reassure** ("We're holding the $385. Nothing's paid until you say it's
done."), **nudge** ("Rate the job? It takes 30 seconds."), **celebrate** ("Sorted.
Wasim's paid and the tap's gone quiet.").

Cheek runs high in marketing headlines, lower in confirmations and empty states, and
**at zero for money, safety and disputes**. There the facts carry the message.

### Starting lengths

These are editing prompts, not quotas. Sometimes "Sorted." is the whole message.

| Element | Aim |
| --- | --- |
| Push notification | Under 60 characters. What happened, then what's next. |
| SMS | Under 160 characters, one message. The trade, the time, the next step. |
| Screen headline | 2–6 words. The state, not the feature. |
| Button | 1–3 words, verb first: Get a quote, Book it. |
| Quote line item | 3–8 words. The part and the work, no codes. |
| Concierge message | One question, under 25 words. Choices where they help. |
| Social caption | 30–70 words. One job, one price or one tip. |

## 2. House style

- **Australian spelling**: colour, organise, **licence** (noun), **license** (verb).
- **Sentence case** for headings, buttons and menus. (Capitals are allowed only for
  the Label style — see typography.)
- Contractions, yes. Numerals in product copy: 3 trades, 2 days.
- **Prices include GST, said once per screen**: `$385 inc. GST`. Cents only when they
  aren't zero: `$82.50`. **Never "from" on a firm quote.**
- Dates and times: `Tue 6 Oct`, `9:30am`. Suburbs by name.
- Write **Sorted By** in prose; the logo is lowercase. Never `SortedBy` or `SORTED BY`.
  When a headline finishes the phrase, the b stays lowercase: *Sorted by Thursday.*
- "We" is Sorted By, "you" is the customer, trades go by their names.
- **The concierge says it's an AI in its first message** and never borrows a person's name.
- One exclamation mark per screen at most — **none in errors, money or safety**.
- **No emoji in product copy.**
- Skip: "she'll be right", ocker costume, startup speak (seamless, hassle-free,
  "Uber for tradies") and fake urgency. Punch at call-out fees and ghosting, never at trades.

### Say / not

| Say | Not |
| --- | --- |
| Firm quote | Estimate, unless it is one |
| We're holding $385 until it's done | Escrow, pre-authorisation |
| Licensed plumber, licence 123456C | Vetted pro, trusted expert |
| Approve the change | Accept variation order |
| Trade, tradie, sparkie, chippie | Service provider, contractor |
| Sorted | Successfully completed |

### The final edit

Can they say what happens next? Is the price in writing, with GST? Is the licence on
show? Would your mum get it on a phone in the sun? Can another word go? Does it sound
like a good tradie on the phone?

## 3. Logo

Phil's thumbs-up house, drawn as one line, is the **b in "by"**. The thumb says
sorted. The house says it's your home we're looking after.

- **The b is the mark.** The thumb is the ascender, the fist is the bowl. The fist sits
  on the baseline and fills the x-height; the thumb rises above the line.
- **The wordmark** is "sorted by" in Bricolage Grotesque Regular, lowercase, with the
  mark as the b.
- **One line, one weight**, round ends, drawn to match the stems of Bricolage Regular.
  **Only the house is Spritz.** Everything else is Ink.
- **The fingers**: four, stacked like courses of brick. The open end on the top finger
  keeps the hand-drawn feel.
- **The house** sits on the fist. Its roof rises from the thumb and its wall drops into
  the first finger. Hand and home are one shape.

### Versions and colourways

| Surface | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| On Plaster and Chalk | Ink line, Spritz house |
| On Ink and Charcoal | Plaster line, Spritz house |
| On Spritz or Brass | All Ink |
| On Gum | All Plaster |
| App icon | Ink hand, **Chalk** house, on Spritz |

The wordmark is the default on screens, signs and print. The mark alone is the app
icon, the avatar, the favicon and the sorted moment.

### Space and sizes

- **Clear space** is the height of the `o` in sorted, on every side. Over photos, set
  it on a Plaster or Ink panel.
- Wordmark **140 px** wide (35 mm print). From **96–139 px** use the small wordmark:
  heavier line, **no chimney**. Mark **48 px** tall; small mark **16–47 px**.
- Never stretch, tilt, outline, shadow or recolour it. Don't fill the house, close the
  open finger or change the line weight. Never type a `b` beside the mark, or use the
  mark as a letter in other words.
- Small versions and app icons are **drawn for their size, not scaled down**.

## 4. Typography

Two typefaces, two jobs. Both are free on Google Fonts under the OFL.

- **Bricolage Grotesque** — headlines, the total and the wordmark. ExtraBold and Bold,
  tight tracking. Regular is kept for the wordmark. 2–6 words a headline.
- **Figtree** — everything people read: body, steps, quote lines, labels and fine print.
  Regular for reading, Medium for small print, SemiBold (650) for emphasis, Bold for
  buttons and capital labels.

Where they can't be installed, use Arial Bold for headlines and Arial for text.
The wordmark is outlined in the logo files; **never retype it**.

| Use | Starting setting |
| --- | --- |
| App display | Bricolage ExtraBold 40/44, tracking −3% |
| Screen title | Bricolage Bold 32/36 |
| Section title | Bricolage Bold 26/32 |
| Total | Bricolage ExtraBold 32/36, tabular, "inc. GST" beside it |
| Body | Figtree Regular 17/26. Never smaller for reading. |
| Small print | Figtree Medium 15/22 |
| Label | Figtree Bold 14 capitals, tracking +6%. Nothing smaller. |
| Social hook (1080×1350) | Bricolage ExtraBold 96–120, 1.05 line height |
| Social body (1080×1350) | Figtree Regular 44–48, 1.25 line height, 80 px margins |

**Keep the character scarce.** One Bricolage headline per screen or card. Conditions,
dates and licence numbers stay in Figtree; the total is the one number in Bricolage.
On phones, display sizes stop at **40 px**. Prices, times and licence numbers use
**tabular figures**. Never squeeze or stretch letters to fit — cut words or split the
screen first.

## 5. Colour

Warm Plaster stays dominant. Ink does the reading. Spritz marks the house and the next step.

| Name | Hex | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spritz | `#F2581E` | The house and the next action |
| Brass | `#DDB95E` | Waiting and money held |
| Ink | `#16130F` | Reading text and the logo line |
| Charcoal | `#2B2825` | Dark surfaces |
| Plaster | `#F6F1E9` | The page |
| Chalk | `#FFFDF9` | Cards and sheets |
| Gum | `#1E6F64` | Done and verified |
| Alarm | `#B8233A` | Safety and errors only |
| Spritz text | `#B5420F` | Orange words under 24 px on Plaster |
| Grey-300 | `#C2BAAF` | Stages still to come |

**Balance:** about 60% Plaster and Chalk, 25% Ink and Charcoal, 10% Spritz, 5% Brass.
Spritz is a highlighter, not wallpaper, except on full-bleed marketing panels.

**Colour means something.** Spritz says do this. Brass says waiting or held. Gum says
done. Alarm says stop. Each status also carries **a word and an icon** — never colour alone.

### Contrast rules (these are constraints, not suggestions)

- **Spritz never warns.** Hazards use Alarm, so orange always means go ahead.
- Ink on Plaster is 16.5:1. Ink on Spritz is 5.5:1.
- **White on Spritz is only 3.4:1, so buttons take Ink text**, not white.
- Spritz words on Plaster (3.0:1) only from 24 px; **smaller orange words use Spritz
  text `#B5420F`** (5.0:1).
- Brass on Plaster is 1.7:1 — **never text**.

## 6. Graphic devices

Four come from the mark; the docket carries every price. Use one per layout.

- **sorted by ___** The wordmark finishes its own sentence: *sorted by Thursday.* The
  ending is Bricolage Bold at the wordmark's size, on its baseline, with a full stop.
  Spritz on light, Brass on dark, Ink on Spritz. Three words at most. A person's name
  only for a licensed trade who has the job.
- **The stack.** The fingers become a progress tracker, one per stage from the bottom:
  firm price, booked, on the way, done. The roof goes on in Spritz when you confirm
  it's done and the trade is paid. Stages to come use grey-300. **Always say the stage
  in words beside it.**
- **The big thumb.** The mark enlarged and cropped at a frame's edge, thumb and house
  in view. Social covers, banners, merch. Pair with a headline, not a second logo.
- **The seal.** A round stamp for promises we can back: firm price, licensed trade,
  paid when it's done. Never under 160 px.
- **The docket.** Every price sits on a docket: notched edges, a dashed tear, the total
  with inc. GST.

## 7. Before it leaves the desk

Check it at 360 px wide and at arm's length. Check spelling, prices with GST, dates,
licence numbers and alt text. Keep the "Design sample" label until real names and
prices are checked. **Every promise must match what the price hold and dispute
process back up.**
